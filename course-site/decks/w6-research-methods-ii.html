SOC 2010 · Introduction to Sociology06 / 15
Qualitative Methods

Designed to capture social life as participants experience it rather than in quantifiable categories predetermined by the researcher

Exploratory in nature

Start with a very broad research question (which may be revised or changed later).

Collect rich/noisy data.

Identify patterns in the data.

Use of narratives rather than statistical inferences to answer the question.

Reflexive research design, in which the design develops as the research progresses

Sensitivity to the subjective role of the researcher

SOC 2010 · Introduction to Sociology12 / 15
Participant Observation

A qualitative method for gathering data in which natural social processes are studied as they happen in the field rather than in the laboratory and left relatively undisturbed

Characteristics

Observing and interacting with subjects in the course of their normal activities

Avoid the artificiality of experiments and the structured questioning of surveys

Context is better understood

SOC 2010 · Introduction to Sociology13 / 15
Ethnography

Ethnography- a naturalistic method based on studying people in their own environment in order to understand the meanings they attribute to their activities; also the written work that results from the study

Ethnographic research is conducted through participant observation- a methodology associated with ethnography whereby the researcher both observes and becomes a member in a social setting

SOC 2010 · Introduction to Sociology14 / 15
Philosophy & Reasoning of Qualitative Methods

Inductive Reasoning:

- A very broad research question (subject to change)

- Collect a large amount of relatively unstructured data

- Identify some patterns in the data.

Interpretivist Philosophy: There is not necessarily an external, objective reality; social realities are constructed by people;

To know such realities researchers need to let people speak for themselves and try to interpret the meanings given to what is observed
