SOC 2010 · Introduction to Sociology09 / 13
Quantitative Research

Methods such as surveys and experiments that record variation in social life in terms of quantities;

Data that are either numbers or attributes that can be ordered in terms of magnitude (i.e., quantifiable).

Use deductive reasoning (deduce new to-be-tested knowledge from existing knowledge);

Hold a positivistic philosophy (there is an objective reality external to us, waiting to be known);

Quantitative methods are most often used when the motives for research are explanation.

The goal of science: To explain and predict based on empirical data (i.e., explanatory)

SOC 2010 · Introduction to Sociology10 / 13
Deductive Reasoning In Quantitative Research: A classical scientific reasoning

Hypotheses deduction: when you form a “tentative answer” (i.e., hypothesis), you are deriving it from an existing general explanation (e.g., theory, common sense, or prior evidence…);

Hypotheses deduced from the general explanation put to test using empirical data;

If the hypotheses were rejected/falsified, then the explanation was rejected; if an explanation was consistently rejected in this manner in different studies, then the explanation is subjected to change (abandoned, or revised);

If the hypotheses were not falsified, then the explanation tentatively hold; if hypotheses consistently failed to be rejected, then the explanation could be theorized (i.e., became a theory).

An Example: hypothetic-deductive reasoning

Prospect theory: people are loss-averse; since individuals dislike losses more than equivalent gains, they are more willing to take actions to avoid a loss than to take actions to get an equivalent gain. This theory can be applied to understand consumers’ psychological responses in the product return context, where consumers once endowed with a product but then considered to give up an ownership of it.

Bundle promotion – jointly frames the main and supplementary items in one account.

Free gift – presents the supplementary item separately from the expenditure for the main product
