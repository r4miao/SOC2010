Course description

“The sociological imagination enables its possessors to understand the interplay of man and society, of biography and history, of self and world.”
— C. Wright Mills

This course introduces you to the discipline of sociology. In this class we will explore how different forms of social forces shape different aspects of our life. We will learn about different theoretical perspectives that sociologists have developed to explain society, as well as conceptual and methodological tools that sociologists use to study social phenomena.

A range of topics will be covered, including but not limited to social structure, social interaction, social inequalities, and a selected number of areas of social life. The goals of this course are not only to acquaint you with basic concepts and theories of sociology, but more importantly, to train your analytical mind to make your own sensible judgments of the world around you. Topics will be examined through lectures, readings, and class discussions.

Learning objectives

After you have completed this course, you should be able to:

  • Identify social forces that affect individuals.
  • Develop a historic overview of the development of the discipline.
  • Understand concepts that are commonly used in the field.
  • Use your “Sociological Imagination” to ask meaningful sociological questions in your everyday life.
  • Apply sociological concepts and/or theoretical perspectives in answering these questions.

Assignments

Quizzes — 40%

There will be five quizzes throughout the semester. Quizzes compose 40 percent of your final grade. Grading will be based on the mean score for all quiz scores. The quizzes will be in the format of multiple-choice questions or true/false questions. Quizzes will not be cumulative; each quiz will cover only the material presented in the segment of the course after the previous quiz. All quizzes must be completed and submitted online through Canvas. Plan to complete quizzes on time. Late submission is not accepted.

Class attendance — 15%

This course will involve a significant amount of in-class discussions. Therefore, continual attendance is critical for successful learning. Students are required to attend all classes through the semester. Attendance will be taken throughout the semester.

Each unexcused absence will result in a 20% discount in the Class Attendance Score, which transfers to a 3% discount of the final grade. Excessive absences, defined as more than five, will lower the final grade by one letter grade, in addition to losing all Class Attendance Score. Students who miss 10 or more class meetings will receive an F for the final grade. Attendance will be taken at the beginning of each class.

Excusable absences include: a certified medical complication; death of an immediate family member; serious illness or emergency in a student’s immediate family; authorized University-sponsored activities; religious observances and practices; court-imposed legal proceedings; required military obligations; and interviews for jobs, co-op assignments, internships, graduate school, or professional school. Documentation may be required, and students should consult the instructor as soon as possible.

Midterm Exam — 15% and Final Exam — 20%

Both exams will cover materials from the textbook, lectures, or other assigned readings. The Final Exam is cumulative and will cover all course materials. The exams are closed-book, so careful study is recommended. Exam dates are listed on the Course Schedule. Make-up exams may be given only for valid excused absences with written documentation. Unless there are major extenuating circumstances, exams must be made up within one week. If you know you will be absent on the day of an exam, notify the instructor as soon as possible.

Research Project — 10%

There will be one research project related to the teaching content. Students who fail to submit on time will receive a zero score. Failing to complete required components of the project will incur a score penalty. Detailed requirements for the project will be posted on Canvas.
